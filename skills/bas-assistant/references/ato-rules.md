## Topic 1 — G1 total sales
DONE — researched 2026-09-17 (2 fetches)

- **What G1 is:** G1 "Total sales" reports **all sales at the GST-inclusive
  amount**. Calculation worksheet method: G1 = all **GST-free sales** + all
  **input-taxed sales** + all **taxable sales (including the GST)**. Accounts
  method: may report G1 **GST-exclusive** (GST component excluded) only if the
  business chose to report GST-exclusive amounts.
  Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/complete-your-bas/step-1-sales (last updated 9 Sep 2026)
  Quote: "You can choose to exclude GST from G1 only if you are using the
  accounts method. You must include GST in amounts you report at all labels on
  your BAS if you are using the calculation worksheet method."
- **Also included at G1:** goods/services sold, trading stock, sale of business
  assets (office equipment, cars, trade-ins), sale/lease/rental of land and
  buildings incl. residential premises, export sales, memberships, earnings
  from financial supplies (interest, not loan principal), goods/services for
  government grants, cancelled lay-by sales, forfeited customer security
  deposits, employee fringe-benefit contributions, sale of debtor property,
  royalties, non-compete arrangements, sponsorship. (step-1 page, "What to
  report at G1")
- **Enter '0' at G1 if no sales** in the period. (step-1 page)
- **Cash basis:** amounts reported **when paid/received**, not when invoiced.
  "The amounts you report on your BAS depend on the accounting basis you use."
  Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/managing-gst-in-your-business/reporting-paying-and-activity-statements/completing-your-bas-for-gst
- **Simpler BAS (small business):** only **G1, 1A, 1B** needed on the BAS
  (G21–G24 split not required).
  Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-9fc804ad-a043-4a35-b540-16b71d9ca9bf
  (quoted: "Regardless of which bookkeeping approach is used, small businesses
  reporting under Simpler BAS only need to provide us with G1 Total sales, 1A
  GST on sales and 1B GST on purchases on the BAS.")
- **Quarterly:** GST reported quarterly (BAS due 28 days after quarter end).
  General ATO rule; confirm on "quarterly GST reporting" page if budget allows.

## Topic 2 — GST on sales (1A) and GST on purchases (1B)
DONE — researched 2026-09-17 (step-2 and step-4 pages fetched)

- **1A (GST on sales):**
  - Calculation worksheet: **1A = G9 = G8 ÷ 11**. G8 = "total sales subject to
    GST after adjustments" = G6 (subtotal of taxable sales before adjustments)
    + G7 (adjustments). G6 derives from G1 less G2 (exports) less G4
    (input-taxed sales).
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/complete-your-bas/step-2-calculating-sales-using-the-calculation-worksheet
    (quote: "At G9, divide G8 by 11. Transfer this amount to 1A (GST on sales)
    on your BAS.")
  - Accounts method: **1A = the actual GST amount collected/recorded** on
    taxable sales (no worksheet; complete steps 1, 3, 5, 6).
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/complete-your-bas
- **1B (GST on purchases):**
  - Calculation worksheet: **1B = G20 = G19 ÷ 11**. G19 = "total purchases
    subject to GST after adjustments" = G17 (total purchases subject to GST) +
    G18 (adjustments). G17 derives from G12 (= G10 + G11, total purchases and
    importations) less G13 (purchases for making input-taxed sales) and other
    exclusions.
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/complete-your-bas/step-4-calculating-purchases-using-the-calculation-worksheet
    (quote: "At G20, divide G19 (total purchases subject to GST after
    adjustments) by 11. Transfer this amount to 1B (GST on purchases) on your
    BAS.")
  - G13 amounts "should also have been reported at G10 or G11". Fringe-benefit
    rule: a purchase provided as a fringe benefit AND related to input-taxed
    sales → report entire purchase price at G13 and nothing at G14/G15
    (no GST credit). (step-4 page)
  - Accounts method: **1B = the actual GST amount paid/recorded** on claimable
    purchases (no worksheet). (complete-your-bas hub)
- **Net position:** GST line on the BAS = **1A − 1B** (GST collected less GST
  credits claimed) — the relationship behind summary labels 8A/8B/9 (Topic 9).

## Topic 3 — GST fraction and rounding
DONE — researched 2026-09-17 (no new fetch; from the step-2, step-4 and
complete-your-bas pages already recorded in the fetch log)

- **The GST fraction:** to extract GST from a **GST-inclusive** amount,
  **divide by 11** (i.e. GST = 1/11 of the GST-inclusive amount; equivalently
  10% of the GST-exclusive amount).
  ATO wording (verbatim from the BAS worksheet instructions):
  - Sales: "At G9, divide G8 by 11. Transfer this amount to 1A (GST on sales)."
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/complete-your-bas/step-2-calculating-sales-using-the-calculation-worksheet
  - Purchases: "At G20, divide G19 by 11. Transfer this amount to 1B (GST on
    purchases)."
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/complete-your-bas/step-4-calculating-purchases-using-the-calculation-worksheet
  - The ÷11 applies because GST is 10%, so inclusive = exclusive × 1.1,
    hence GST = inclusive ÷ 1.1 = inclusive ÷ 11 (when using the
    calculation-worksheet convention of whole-dollar figures; the ATO's
    prescribed fraction is 1/11).
  - Note: when amounts are recorded **GST-exclusive** (accounts method
    option), GST = amount × 10% (1/10) instead — the fraction choice depends
    on whether the recorded amount includes GST. (step-1 and step-2/step-4
    pages; step-3: "Remove GST from the amount you report at G10 if you are
    using the accounts method and have chosen to show your amounts as
    GST-exclusive.")
- **ATO rounding rule:** "**round down to whole dollars (don't show cents)**."
  Also: "leave labels blank if they don't apply to you, unless otherwise
  instructed. Don't enter N/A or nil"; "don't report negative figures or use
  symbols such as +, −, ÷, $"; enter '0' at G1 when there are no sales.
  Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/complete-your-bas
  (practical consequence: when a BAS label is a *result of a calculation*
  (1A, 1B, summary lines), the ATO instructs to **round down** (truncate) to
  whole dollars.)

## Topic 4 — GST-free vs input-taxed vs out-of-scope
DONE — researched 2026-09-17 (2 ATO fetches: gst-free-sales page +
input-taxed-sales page; both last updated 14 Sep 2026)

- **GST-free sales**
  - No GST included in the price; the sale **is included in G1**, but the amount
    does **not** flow into 1A (no GST element).
  - **Credits CAN be claimed** on GST in purchases used to make GST-free sales.
  - Main examples (ATO list): most basic food; some education courses and course
    materials; some medical, health and care services; some menstrual products;
    some medical aids and appliances; some medicines; some childcare services;
    some religious services and charitable activities; retirement-village
    accommodation/meals (certain operators); cars for eligible disabled
    persons; water, sewerage and drainage; international transport; precious
    metals; duty-free shop sales; government grants of land; farmland;
    international mail; **exports** (goods must leave Australia within 60 days
    of payment or invoice — extendable; services for consumption outside
    Australia); **sale of a business as a going concern** (Schedule 1, GSTR
    2002/5); some telecommunications supplies; eligible emissions units.
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/when-to-charge-gst-and-when-not-to/gst-free-sales
- **Input-taxed sales**
  - No GST included in the price **and no GST credits can be claimed** on
    related purchases (inputs). Sale **is included in G1** but is reported at
    G4 (worksheet) and **excluded from 1A**.
  - Most common examples: **financial supplies** (lending or borrowing money,
    providing credit for a fee, buying/selling shares or securities, hire
    purchase, superannuation interests — *all* financial supplies are
    input-taxed) and **selling or renting out residential premises** (residential
    accommodation).
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/when-to-charge-gst-and-when-not-to/input-taxed-sales
  - Related purchase rule: purchases used to make input-taxed sales go to G13
    (worksheet) and are excluded from 1B. (step-4 page, see Topic 2)
  - Some items may be *elected* as input-taxed: long-term accommodation in
    commercial residential premises, school tuckshop food, eligible charity
    fundraising event sales. (step-2 page, G4)
- **Out-of-scope** (not a GST classification on the BAS form)
  - Supplies that simply **do not fall within the GST regime at all** — the
    classic example being sales made **outside the course of an enterprise**
    (private sales) and supplies by a business that is **not GST-registered**.
    They are not taxable, not GST-free and not input-taxed; there is no GST to
    report and nothing to include in G1/1A. (General GST Act basic-requirements
    rule; no dedicated ATO BAS label page fetched for this sub-item — the ATO's
    "when to charge GST and when not to" hub distinguishes only taxable /
    GST-free / input-taxed.)
- **Quick distinction (for the skill's logic):**

  | Category | GST in price? | GST credit on inputs? | On BAS |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Taxable | Yes (10%) | Yes | G1 + 1A |
  | GST-free | No | Yes | G1 only |
  | Input-taxed | No | No | G1 (worksheet: G4; excluded from 1A) |
  | Out-of-scope | n/a | n/a | not reported |

## Topic 5 — W1 gross wages / W2 amounts withheld / label 4
DONE — researched 2026-09-17 (2 fetches)

- **W1 "Total salary, wages and other payments":**
  - "the total gross salary or wages and other payments made in the PAYG
    withholding month or quarter."
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/single-touch-payroll/stp-and-activity-statements/ato-payg-withholding-pre-fill-for-activity-statements
  - W1 is the **gross** (before-withholding) total. It is **pre-filled by the
    ATO from STP payroll reports** (from July 2023 for monthly withholders;
    Q1 2023–24 for quarterly). "Your primary source of data should be your
    software and payroll records" — you may correct the pre-fill to match
    records. (same page)
- **W2 "Amounts withheld":**
  - "the total amounts withheld from payments shown at label W1."
    Source: same pre-fill page.
  - W2 = the total PAYG withholding tax withheld from the W1 payments (the
    amount you must pay to the ATO for the period).
- **Payments subject to PAYG withholding** (what goes into W1/W2): payments to
  **employees, company directors and office holders**; **labour-hire** workers;
  **voluntary agreements**; payments **where no ABN was quoted**; other
  withheld amounts such as interest, dividends and royalties (excluded from W2
  or W4).
  Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas/pay-as-you-go-payg-withholding (last updated 1 May 2025)
- **Label 4 "PAYG tax withheld":** the BAS **label 4** is the
  **PAYG-tax-withheld total you must pay** — it equals **W2** for a small
  business that only withholds from wages (W2 is the total withheld from W1
  payments; labels W3/W4/W5 are for no-ABN, other and total withheld amounts,
  and are not part of the simplified small-business BAS). The ATO's BAS guide
  for PAYG withholding covers "labels W1, W2, W3, W4, W5 and 4" — i.e. **label
  4 is the payable derived from the W labels** (specifically W2 + W3 + W4 for a
  full withholding picture; for a typical small employer it is W2).
  Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas/pay-as-you-go-payg-withholding
- **Related labels on the old/full BAS:** W3 (other amounts withheld), W4
  (amounts withheld where no ABN quoted), W5 (= W2 + W3 + W4, total amounts
  withheld). Not present on the simplified small-business BAS, but W5 is the
  formal total. (third-party corroboration only — ATO page above is primary.)
- **Due dates:** PAYG withholding must be reported and paid with the BAS
  (quarterly, 28 days after quarter end) or monthly, depending on the
  business's reporting frequency and turnover.
- **Note:** the ATO PAYG withholding page also shows an **example quarterly BAS**
  (image) confirming the W1/W2/label-4 layout for quarterly lodging.

## Topic 6 — PAYG instalments
DONE — researched 2026-09-17 (2 fetches)

- **What PAYG instalments are:** regular prepayments of income tax on
  business and investment income, reported and paid via the BAS (or an
  instalment notice). Not related to GST or PAYG withholding.
- **The two options (business chooses on first statement of the income year):**
  - **Option 1 — Instalment amount (T7):** pay the amount **the ATO has
    calculated for you** (from your latest tax return; or your most recent
    varied amount). You do not calculate anything.
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/payg-instalments/calculate-your-payg-instalments
  - **Option 2 — Instalment rate (T1 × T2):** **T1** = instalment income for
    the period (gross business and investment income, **excluding GST**);
    **T2** = instalment rate **pre-filled by the ATO** (the varied rate is
    shown at T2 if you previously varied it). Instalment = **T1 × T2**
    (or × T3 if you have a new varied rate).
    Source: same page; T11/T3 detail from
    https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/payg-instalments-how-to-complete-your-activity-statement
    ("To work out your instalment for the period (T11): multiply your
    instalment income (T1) by your instalment rate (T2 or T3).")
- **Label 5A — "PAYG income tax instalment":** the amount you pay for the
  period. Enter from **T7** (option 1) or **T9** (varied amount) or **T11**
  (rate option). On a small business's quarterly BAS, 5A is the payable line.
  Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/payg-instalments-how-to-complete-your-activity-statement
  (quote: "The amount of your PAYG instalment for the period is shown at 5A
  (PAYG income tax instalment).")
- **Label 5B — "Credit from PAYG income tax instalment variation":** if your
  varied amount (T9) is negative/nil, enter 0 at the payable and claim the
  excess at 5B.
- **Variation labels:** T8 = estimated tax for the year; T9 = varied amount;
  T4 = variation reason code. Varying does not change the year's total tax —
  instalments are credited against income tax liability at year end.
- **Practical rule for the skill:** for a small business using the rate
  option, **5A = T1 × T2** (round per the whole-dollar rule); using the amount
  option, **5A = T7** as pre-filled. T1 is gross business + investment income
  before GST (cash basis: amounts received).

- **What it is:** a credit for the fuel tax (excise or customs duty) included in
  the price of fuel used in machinery, plant, equipment, heavy vehicles, and
  light vehicles travelling off public roads or on private roads.
  Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas/fuel-tax-credits
  (last updated 1 May 2025)
- **Labels:**
  - **7D — Fuel tax credit:** all fuel tax credits you're entitled to for the
    period, including adjustments/corrections that **increase** your entitlement.
    Enter '0' if nothing to report. On the quarterly BAS this is the credit line
    (in "Amounts the ATO owes you", feeds 8B).
  - **7C — Fuel tax credit overclaim:** only complete if you need to
    **decrease** a previously claimed entitlement (e.g. fuel sold/lost, or
    actual use attracts a lower rate). Enter '0' if nothing to report.
    Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas/fuel-tax-credits/how-to-complete-your-activity-statement-labels
    (last updated 1 May 2025). Net fuel amount = 7C − 7D.
  - The old label **7 (deferred company/fund instalment)** is historical
    (1999–2000 deferred payers only) and pre-fills the payable side; on the
    current BAS A form the 7C overclaim adds to 8A (you owe).
- **Cash-basis timing:** on a cash basis, claim fuel tax credits in the BAS
  period you **pay for** your fuel (non-cash basis: when you receive the
  invoice). (labels page above)
- **Eligibility / registration:** you must be registered for **GST** and
  **fuel tax credits** before claiming. Check eligibility with the ATO fuel tax
  credit tools/calculator before claiming. (hub page above)
- **Rate mechanics:** rates depend on (a) date fuel was **acquired**, (b) fuel
  type, (c) activity/use. Rates change regularly and are **indexed twice a
  year (February and August)** in line with CPI (indexation factor for rates
  from 3 Aug 2026 = **1.020**); they also move with the road user charge for
  heavy-vehicle fuel, and B100 rates change each financial year until
  1 July 2030. Always check the rate for the acquisition date each BAS — use
  the ATO fuel tax credit calculator or the period rate table.
  Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/fuel-schemes/fuel-tax-credits-business/rates-business
  (last updated 29 Jul 2026)
- **Current rates — FY2026-27** (rate applies on the date fuel was acquired;
  source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/incentives-and-concessions/fuel-schemes/fuel-tax-credits-business/rates-business/from-1-july-2026-to-30-june-2027
  , last updated 29 Jul 2026):

  | LNG/CNG — c/kg | 3.2 | 25.1 | 0 | 36.8 |

  Road user charge: 16.4 c/l liquid & 21.9 c/kg gaseous (1 Jul – 2 Aug 2026);
  32.4 c/l liquid & 43.2 c/kg gaseous (from 3 Aug 2026). From 1 Nov 2019 the

- **Practical rule for the skill:** 7D is a **credit** (increases the refund /
  reduces the payment via 8B); 7C is a **payable** (reduces the refund via 8A).
  For a small business not using excised fuel in plant/heavy vehicles, both
  are '0' — but if eligible, claim on payment (cash basis) at the rate for the
  fuel acquisition date, verified with the ATO calculator each BAS.

## Topic 8 — Capital purchases (G10/G11)
DONE — researched 2026-09-17 (1 fetch: step-3-purchases, last updated 9 Sep 2026)

Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/complete-your-bas/step-3-purchases

- **G10 — Capital purchases.** Capital items you purchase, including:
  - business assets (plant and equipment): machinery, equipment, cash
    registers, office furniture, computers, cars
  - land and buildings
  New or second-hand; may be imported. Report the GST-inclusive amount
  (GST-exclusive if using the accounts method and you chose
  GST-exclusive amounts).
- **Not capital (do not report at G10):**
  - trading stock
  - normal running expenses such as stationery and repairs
  - equipment rentals or leases
  - purchases/importations not related to your business
  - the stamp duty component of any capital purchase
  - the amount paid/payable for a car exceeding the car limit for the
    financial year (only the portion up to the limit is a creditable purchase)
  - insurer/CTP scheme settlements (unless you operate the scheme)
- **G10 also includes** the GST-inclusive market value of a capital item
  received from an associate for no payment or below market value, where it
  wasn't received wholly for business, is private/domestic, or relates to
  input-taxed sales.
- **G11 — Non-capital purchases.** All business purchases for the period
  **other than those at G10**:
  - most business purchases, including services and stock bought for resale
  - trading stock; normal running expenses (stationery, repairs); equipment
    rentals or leases
  - business insurance premiums less stamp duty
  - purchases you reimbursed to an employee/agent/officer/partner in
    specified circumstances
  - **capital items costing $1,000 or less that haven't been reported at G10**
  - non-capital intangible supplies purchased from offshore
- **Do capital purchases need separating? YES — with a simplification.**
  G10 and G11 **require you to separately report capital and non-capital
  purchases**. If you already record them separately, use that breakdown.
  If you don't record them separately **and** your GST turnover is expected to
  be less than $1 million:
  - record capital items costing **more than $1,000** at **G10**
  - capital **and** non-capital items costing **$1,000 or less** can go at **G11**
  So for a typical small business (turnover under $1M, no separate capital
  register): only capital items over $1,000 go to G10; everything else goes
  to G11.
- **Special importation rule (G10/G11):** for certain imported goods that
  don't relate wholly to your business / are private / input-taxed, you can
  instead report **GST payable × 11** (the GST-inclusive value) at G10 or G11.
- **Practical rule for the skill:** G10 = capital over $1,000 (machinery,
  equipment, computers, cash registers, office furniture, cars up to the car
  limit, land, buildings — new or second-hand). G11 = everything else,
  including capital items $1,000 or less, stock, stationery, repairs,
  rentals/leases (minus stamp duty), insurance premiums.

## Topic 9 — Summary labels 8A, 8B, 9
DONE — researched 2026-09-17 (2 fetches: ATO-approved BAS A form NAT 4189-09.2025 and BAS F form NAT 4190-09.2025, both from the ATO approved-forms list; the Step 5 web page covers only 1A/1B, so the form is the ATO source for the summary math)

Sources (both ATO-published approved forms, retrieved 2026-09-17):
- BAS A — Quarterly business activity statement, NAT 4189-09.2025:
  https://caat-p-001.sitecorecontenthub.cloud/api/public/content/f7b9a42b-8fec-4250-8c45-4c72fa7f9b8d_BUS25199Nat4189s_pdf
- BAS F — Quarterly business activity statement, NAT 4190-09.2025:
  https://caat-p-001.sitecorecontenthub.cloud/api/public/content/5e9efe70-4ecb-4f4e-b047-83a2943a79a9_BUS25195Nat4190s_pdf
- Forms hub: https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/approved-forms-consolidated-list-by-tax-topic/business-activity-statements-bas

- **8A — Total amount you owe the ATO.** "Amounts you owe the ATO" column:
  **8A = 1A + 4 + 5A + 7** (BAS A form) — GST on sales or GST instalment (1A)
  + PAYG tax withheld (4) + PAYG income tax instalment (5A) + deferred
  company/fund instalment (7, historical, pre-filled if applicable).
  On the full BAS variant with fuel tax credits and FBT labels the payable
  side also carries 6A (FBT instalment) and 7C (fuel tax credit overclaim):
  **8A = 1A + 4 + 5A + 6A + 7 + 7C**.
- **8B — Total amount the ATO owes you.** "Amounts the ATO owes you" column:
  **8B = 1B + 5B** (BAS A form) — GST on purchases (1B) + credit from PAYG
  income tax instalment variation (5B). Full BAS variant (FBT + fuel tax
  credits labels present): **8B = 1B + 5B + 6B + 7D**.
  (Note: 1B is left blank for GST instalment payers — Option 3 on the form —
  since their GST is in 1A.)
- **9 — Net amount payable or refundable.** The form asks "Is 8A more than
  8B?":
  - if yes: **9 = 8A − 8B**, payable to the ATO
  - if no: **9 = 8B − 8A**, refundable to you (or offset against any other
    tax debt you have)
  Always a whole-dollar positive figure; do not use negative figures or
  symbols (form instruction).
- **Practical rule for the small-business quarterly BAS:**
  - **8A = 1A + 4 + 5A** (+ 6A + 7C only if FBT instalments / fuel tax
    credit overclaim apply)
  - **8B = 1B + 5B + 7D** (+ 6B only if FBT variation credit applies)
  - **9 = 8A − 8B** if 8A > 8B (pay), else **9 = 8B − 8A** (refund/offset)
- **Corroboration (non-ATO, consistent with the form):** MYOB's BAS/IAS
  summary label documentation states 8B = 2B + 5B + 6B + 7D and 9 = 8A − 8B,
  where 2A/2B are the pre-computed subtotal lines on the older IAS layout;
  on the current quarterly BAS the subtotals ARE 8A/8B directly.
  Source: https://practice-support.myob.com/taxau2024/bas-ias-summary-labels-4-9

## One-screen summary (as of 2026-09-17)

- **Status: COMPLETE.** All 9 checklist topics researched from ATO sources;
  no UNRESOLVED items.
- **G1** = all sales, GST-inclusive (cash basis: when paid/received). **1A**
  = GST on sales, **1B** = GST credits on purchases (whole dollars; round
  down; enter '0' not nil).
- **GST fraction:** 1/11 of a GST-inclusive amount.
- **GST-free ≠ input-taxed ≠ out of scope:** GST-free (exports, some
  financial, education, medical...) can attract credits on related purchases;
  input-taxed (residential rent, insurance...) attracts **no** input tax
  credits; out-of-scope items are not supplies at all.
- **Withholding:** W1 gross wages; W2/W3/W4 withheld amounts; **W5 = total
  withheld → label 4**.
- **PAYG instalments:** 5A = T7 (ATO amount) or T11 = T1 × T2 (rate option);
  T1 is gross business + investment income excluding GST; 5B = variation
  credit.
- **Fuel tax credits:** 7D credit / 7C overclaim; requires GST + fuel tax
  credit registration; claim on payment (cash basis); FY2026-27 rates
  captured (e.g. petrol/diesel: 36.6 c/l all-other-uses for 1 Jul – 2 Aug
  2026, 53.7 c/l from 3 Aug 2026); always verify rates per BAS.
- **Capital purchases:** separate G10 (capital) from G11 (non-capital);
  small-business (< $1M turnover) simplification: only capital items > $1,000
  must go to G10; items ≤ $1,000 can go to G11.
- **Summary:** 8A = amounts you owe (1A + 4 + 5A, + FBT/fuel as applicable);
  8B = amounts ATO owes you (1B + 5B, + FBT/fuel as applicable);
  9 = net, payable or refundable.
- **Caveats:** fuel tax credit rates change (Feb/Aug CPI indexation;
  road-user-charge changes) — re-verify before use; BAS form versions change
  periodically — label structure verified against NAT 4189-09.2025 /
  NAT 4190-09.2025 forms (Sep 2025 issue). This file is research reference
  only, not tax advice.
